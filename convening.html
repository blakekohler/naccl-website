National Convening — December 8

Tuesday, December 8, 2026 · Hilton San Diego Bayfront, San Diego, California · 9:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m. Pacific · In person

We have work to do together

A stronger profession. A trusted peer community. A national voice. The national convening brings the full membership together around those three purposes — and puts them to work.

This is a working convening. The morning starts with current events and their impact on local systems, turns those into focused problem statements, and seats NACCL's inaugural Board of Directors. In the afternoon, every member joins one of six work sessions to begin a practical resource, project outline, or issue brief — with first-90-day steps and volunteers to carry it forward. Together, members will identify up to three priorities for 2027 and a foundation for 2028.

Registration is $100. Optional check-in and coffee begin at 8:30 a.m., lunch is provided from noon to 1:00 p.m., and the program ends at 3:00 p.m. Who should attend: NACCL member CoC executives, senior leaders, and designated organizational representatives — 100+ participants anticipated. You do not need a board seat to help lead this work.

Agenda

All times Pacific. Please plan travel around participation in the full program.

  • 8:30–9:00 a.m.

    Optional check-in & coffee

    Arrive, meet fellow CoC leaders, and settle in before the program begins.

  • 9:00–9:20 a.m.

    Welcome: why NACCL, why now

    Connect the three purposes to the work ahead, and the ways members can lead — through the board, National Practice Councils, Policy Forums, and shared projects.

  • 9:20–10:15 a.m.

    The moment we are in

    A brief factual overview and table discussions on current developments, local impacts, and the shared needs that call for common practice, peer support, policy analysis, or collective representation.

  • 10:30–11:15 a.m.

    Define the work

    Turn the morning's themes into focused problem statements for the afternoon work sessions, and connect them to NACCL's first-year priorities.

  • 11:15 a.m.–12:00 p.m.

    Seating the Board of Directors

    Complete the board-seating process under NACCL's adopted bylaws and introduce the inaugural board. Formal voting is reserved for eligible voting representatives.

  • 12:00–1:00 p.m.

    Lunch & connection

    Lunch provided. A full hour for conversation and a genuine pause — no speakers, presentations, or facilitated work.

  • 1:00–2:00 p.m.

    Member work sessions

    Six concurrent sessions. Take one theme from the morning and begin a draft resource, project outline, or issue brief.

  • 2:15–2:45 p.m.

    From work products to our first-year direction

    Brief reports from each session. Identify up to three priorities for 2027, opportunities to combine efforts, and work to sequence for 2028.

  • 2:45–3:00 p.m.

    Closing

    Next steps, volunteer participation, and how members will receive work products and progress updates. Adjourn at 3:00 p.m.

Choose your work session

All six sessions run concurrently from 1:00 to 2:00 p.m. Select one and stay with it for the hour. Each group takes one theme from the morning, begins a useful work product, and identifies first-90-day steps, volunteer contributors, resources needed, a progress measure, and a proposed follow-up date.

New CoC lead & staff orientation

National Practice Council. What should new CoC leaders and lead-agency staff know in their first 90 days? Begins a starter orientation outline and a first-90-day learning map.

A shared resource toolkit

National Practice Council. Which templates, sample documents, and practical tools would be most useful? Begins initial toolkit categories and a submission-and-review checklist.

Training & professional development

National Practice Council. What learning do current leaders and staff need most? Begins three training priorities and an outline for one initial learning session.

CoC Interim Rule & federal policy

Policy Forum. For self-identified policy wonks, ready to work in the details. Begins an issue brief outline with source references, evidence gaps, and next analysis steps.

Organizing our national voice

Advocacy Gathering. How should NACCL gather local experience and coordinate federal engagement? Begins a member-to-NACCL communication workflow and a factual briefing template.

CFOs & other lead-agency roles

National Practice Council. What support do finance, operations, and other specialized staff need? Begins a pilot peer-group outline, such as a CFO circle.

These are starting work sessions, not a commitment to launch six permanent bodies. Continuing councils, forums, work plans, and resources will follow NACCL's adopted governance, and draft outputs require review before they are issued in NACCL's name.
