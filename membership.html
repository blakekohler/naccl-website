Membership in NACCL

Membership is held at the CoC level, and every member CoC has one vote regardless of size or dues tier.

Why it matters

CoCs coordinate the housing, data, and service networks their communities rely on — yet until now, CoC leaders have not had a national professional association of their own.

NACCL changes that. It is a professional association whose members are CoCs, governed by CoC leaders — created to strengthen leadership capacity, connect peers across communities, and support the work of the field nationwide.

While membership is held by the CoC and one membership covers the whole team, NACCL's focus is the people who lead and coordinate that work. It is a professional association for CoC leaders, not a general membership body for everyone connected to a CoC.

Who can join

Any HUD-designated Continuum of Care. Membership is held by the CoC, not by an individual or a single agency, and your CoC decides locally which entity enrolls, pays dues, and designates the voting representative: a collaborative applicant, a city or county department, a housing authority, or the governance body itself. One membership covers the whole team, and one vote is cast through the designated representative.

Dues

Dues are calculated on an eight-tier scale keyed to your CoC's HUD Estimated Annual Renewal Demand, a figure HUD publishes for every CoC. Larger systems pay more, and every CoC still gets the same single vote. CoCs with zero Annual Renewal Demand may participate without dues while they stabilize.

Paying for membership

That is a local decision. Use an allowable fund source and follow your own approval, procurement, and grants-compliance requirements, in consultation with your fiscal agent, collaborative applicant leadership, or local counsel. Virginia and Maryland worked within their networks and decided to cover dues statewide, through Virginia Housing and the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development.

Joining after the founding window

Membership is open to every HUD-designated CoC. A CoC that joins and is current on its dues before voting opens can take part in the first Board election, so there is still time for your CoC to help choose the first board. Founding status itself closed with the window on July 31, 2026, and will not reopen.

What membership is for

Professional development

Support for the people who lead and coordinate Continuums of Care.

Peer learning

More regular ways to connect with people doing the same work, so what works in one community reaches another.

Shared resources

Practical tools, shared information, and materials CoCs can own and maintain together.

A collective voice in federal policy

NACCL can take positions on federal issues identified by its members without asking any one CoC or lead agency to take that on.

Governance

Nominations for the first elected Board of Directors open this fall. Every HUD region and every CoC type holds seats, so small, rural, and balance of state CoCs all have a voice at the table. Four standing committees run alongside the board, all open to members: Advocacy Steering, Finance, Governance and Nominating, and Membership and Engagement.

Any member CoC that is current on its dues when voting opens may vote, through the representative it has designated. If your CoC has not named a representative, that is the thing to take care of before nominations open.
